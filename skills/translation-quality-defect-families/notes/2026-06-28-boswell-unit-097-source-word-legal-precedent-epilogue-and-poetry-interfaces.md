# Boswell Unit 097: Source-Word, Legal Precedent, Epilogue, and Poetry Interfaces

## Scope

Use this note when translating or reviewing eighteenth-century English biography
annotations that combine source-word glosses, legal precedent, theatre history,
and verse criticism.

## Defect Family

1. Source-word discussion can become opaque if the translation preserves only
   the English word. Terms such as `conceits`, `mixed wit`, `indifferently`,
   and `without concern` need a Chinese gloss while keeping the source token
   visible when the note is about that token.
2. Legal precedent notes need event and procedure precision. Phrases like
   `attainted`, `arraigned on his original sentence`, and identity-only trial
   should not be flattened into a generic new trial.
3. Theatre terms and nicknames need reader-facing interfaces. `Epilogue`,
   `Namby Pamby`, and play/source titles should keep enough source form to
   support later cross-reference, but not leave the Chinese reader without a
   usable term.
4. Quoted poetry criticism can become stiff when eighteenth-century poetic
   terms are translated mechanically. `numbers` in praise of verse usually
   calls for a poetic rendering such as rhythm, verse movement, or poetic
   cadence, not a mathematical-sounding literal.

## Review Checks

- For each italicized or quoted source word, ask whether the note explains that
  word. If yes, keep the source word and add a compact Chinese gloss.
- For legal-historical notes, separate conviction/status, custody/escape,
  later capture, procedure, and precedent; do not merge them into one generic
  judicial action.
- For theatre and periodical titles, preserve the first source-name interface
  when the title may recur in notes or indexes.
- For verse praise, read the Chinese aloud; replace over-literal critical terms
  that sound like technical measurement rather than literary evaluation.
