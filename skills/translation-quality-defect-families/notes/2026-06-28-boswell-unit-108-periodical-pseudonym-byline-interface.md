# Boswell Unit 108: Periodical Pseudonym Byline Interface

## Context

Eighteenth-century periodicals and editorial notes often cite a paper with a formula such as `The World, by Adam Fitz-Adam`. The `by` phrase may identify a house pseudonym, editorial mask, or byline convention rather than a real-name author. Unit 108 exposed this risk in the note on *The World*.

## Defect Pattern

- Translating `by Adam Fitz-Adam` as direct authorship can mislead readers into treating a pseudonymous periodical persona as a historical author.
- The error is especially likely in bibliographical notes, where compact English source formulas combine title, pseudonym, date range, editor, and contributors.

## Prevention Checklist

- In periodical notes, distinguish title, byline/pseudonym, editor, and contributors before translating `by`.
- Prefer Chinese interfaces such as `署名...`, `以...名义`, or `由...编辑` according to evidence.
- If the same note separately names an editor or contributors, do not let the byline phrase absorb those roles.
- Record the correction in chapter controls when the source formula itself is part of bibliographical evidence.

## Applied In Unit 108

- Revised `The World, by Adam Fitz-Adam` from an authorship-like translation to `《世界》署名亚当·菲茨-亚当`.
- Verified the same note still separately names Edward Moore as editor and Chesterfield/Corke/Walpole/Cambridge/Jenyns as contributors.
