# 18th-Century Biography: Youthful Verse and Satirical Artifice

## Scope

Use this note when reviewing quoted youthful verse, school exercises, theatre
epilogues, or social satire embedded inside an eighteenth-century biography.

## Defect Families

- Fame and posterity language can look monetary. In authorial ambition poems,
  phrases such as `future millions` may mean future multitudes of readers or
  posterity, not future money.
- Publication phrases such as `flies to press` usually describe rushing into
  print or publication. Avoid a mechanical image of running to a printing
  machine unless the source is literally about machinery.
- Satirical lists of feminine artifice require social-object reading. `ivory
  teeth` in this register points to artificial teeth, and `whalebones` points
  to stays or corset supports, not pretty teeth or literal bones as weapons.
- Verse headings and character names still need note-marker and proper-noun
  control, but the translated heading should remain readable as a quoted poem
  or dramatic item rather than a glossary dump.

## Review Checklist

- Reconcile every note marker in verse headings before checking the prose.
- Identify whether nouns in comic/satirical lists are literal objects,
  fashion artifices, pets, social habits, or mythic figures.
- Read the poem once as Chinese verse without source support. It may remain
  archaic and formal, but it must not carry a false concrete image.
- Any correction to a poem image, social-object term, or note placement makes
  that review round `FIXED_RECHECK_REQUIRED`; rerun the full-chapter pass before
  PASS.
