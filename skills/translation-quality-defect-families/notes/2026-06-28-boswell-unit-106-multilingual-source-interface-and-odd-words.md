# Boswell Unit 106: Multilingual Source Interfaces And Odd Words

## Context

Boswell's note units can mix Latin epitaphs, classical Latin quotations, English verse translations, French lexical residue, and editorial oddities inside one chapter. In unit 106 the risks clustered around Elizabeth Johnson's Latin epitaph, Creech's English rendering of Horace, Johnson's italicized `qualitied`, Hawkins's `deshabille`, and Croker's `Mrs. [? Mr.]` bracket.

## Defect Pattern

- Keeping a non-English quoted passage verbatim without a nearby Chinese rendering leaves the Chinese reader with an untranslated content block.
- Flattening source-visible odd words such as italicized `qualitied` into smooth Chinese loses the editor's evidence and the author's lexical texture.
- Editorial uncertainty markers such as `Mrs. [? Mr.]` should not be silently normalized; the uncertainty is part of the scholarly interface.

## Prevention Checklist

- For every retained Latin, French, Greek, or English source quotation, decide whether the reader also needs a Chinese rendering. If the passage carries content rather than merely naming a title, add one.
- Preserve source-visible odd words beside a short Chinese gloss when the source word itself is being quoted, italicized, corrected, or discussed.
- Keep bracketed editorial corrections visible in Chinese, using compact forms such as `〔？应作...〕`.
- In post-translation review, scan for italicized source words and all-caps editor labels before declaring PASS.

## Applied In Unit 106

- Added a Chinese rendering after the Latin epitaph while preserving the inscription.
- Added a Chinese rendering for Creech's English line.
- Preserved `qualitied` and `deshabille` with Chinese glosses.
- Clarified `Mrs. [? Mr.] Alderman Sawbridge` as `夫人〔？应作先生〕`.
