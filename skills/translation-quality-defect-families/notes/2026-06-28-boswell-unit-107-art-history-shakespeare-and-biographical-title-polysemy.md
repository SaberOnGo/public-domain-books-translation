# Boswell Unit 107: Art-History, Shakespeare, And Biographical-Title Polysemy

## Context

Boswell's annotation units often quote later editors, art historians, Shakespeare, periodical bibliographers, and estate records in compressed form. Unit 107 exposed several words whose modern default Chinese rendering would be misleading: Raphael's `cartoon`, Shakespeare's `purge` and `sack`, and `two quarto volumes of his Life`.

## Defect Pattern

- Art-history `cartoon` can be mistranslated as modern `卡通画`; in eighteenth- and nineteenth-century art writing it may mean a large preparatory design or tapestry cartoon.
- Shakespearean or early-modern food and drink words should not be flattened into generic modern terms. `sack` is a fortified wine, and `purge` in the Falstaff line means taking a purge.
- Biographical manuscript phrases such as `his Life` may mean a life-record or autobiographical material, not necessarily a formal book title.

## Prevention Checklist

- In notes that cite paintings, engravings, cartoons, or old collections, test visual-art terms against art-history senses before choosing modern everyday Chinese.
- In Shakespeare, early-modern drama, drinking songs, or dictionary entries, preserve the source interface when the word itself is quoted, and give a precise Chinese rendering rather than a generic paraphrase.
- Before marking an English noun phrase as a book title with Chinese title brackets, confirm that the source typography and sentence function support title status.
- In chapter controls, list these as polysemy watch items even if they appear in short notes rather than main narrative.

## Applied In Unit 107

- Revised Raphael-context `cartoon` to `巨幅画稿`.
- Revised `purge` / `sack` to `服泻药` / `萨克酒`.
- Revised `two quarto volumes of his Life` to `他记录自己生平的两卷四开本`.
